A3  GAMEABILITY — is the score measuring modelling, or form-filling?
------------------------------------------------------------------------
  rich reading (3 agents, real content) : 0.8991
  VACUOUS   ("." in every field)        : 0.0850
  deep but narrow (1 agent, precise)    : 0.3607

  rich == vacuous, exactly?             : no   (delta 0.814100)
  vacuous beats the deep reading by     : -0.2757  (0.24x)

  A content-free extraction no longer scores like a real one: "." in
  every field scores 0.0850 against 0.8991 for the rich reading.
  But the floors that separate them are SYNTACTIC — a character class,
  minimum lengths, a placeholder list. They tell "filled with a
  placeholder" from "filled"; they cannot tell a plausible INVENTED
  extraction from a true one. The score is schema coverage with a
  syntactic floor, not modelling quality, and the producer is still an
  LLM asked to fill this exact schema.

  The direction of the bias is the problem. If completeness is really a
  proxy for schema-filling eagerness, then a correlation with downstream
  fidelity is exactly what you would ALSO see if eager runs were simply
  the runs on richer dockets. The claim needs the confound ruled out;
  nothing in this repo rules it out.

A4  RESOLUTION — how finely can this score rank ten cases?
------------------------------------------------------------------------
  distinct values reachable at all      : 363
  distinct values in the plausible band : 144
  that band spans                       : 0.2757 .. 0.9276


  P(at least one tie among n=10)        : 0.2736   under a UNIFORM draw
  That figure is a LOWER BOUND, and deliberately so. The extractor does
  not sample the band uniformly — an LLM told to fill a schema fills most
  of it, so the realised distribution is bunched near the top and ties are
  strictly more likely than this. What the number establishes is that ties
  are not a hypothetical: even at their least likely they occur about 27%
  of the time. A Spearman rho is a statistic on RANKS, and ties inflate or
  deflate it depending on how they are broken. The reported +0.61 is quoted
  to two decimals with no tie correction stated and no n=10 table to
  inspect, so its tie structure is unknown and its third digit is not
  recoverable from anything in this repository.

  "+0.61 vs +0.08 — a 7x lift" is a ratio of two point estimates whose
  intervals both straddle zero and overlap each other across most of
  their length. A ratio of two quantities that may both be zero is not a
  lift; 7x is an artefact of dividing by a small noisy denominator. Had
  the trajectory figure landed at +0.02 the same data would have read as
  "a 30x lift", and at -0.01 the ratio would have changed sign.
